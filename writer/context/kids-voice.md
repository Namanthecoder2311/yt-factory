## Who I am talking to
Two people at once:
1. **The child (ages 1–5)**, who watches, sings along and copies actions.
2. **The parent**, who picks the video, sits nearby and decides if it is safe to replay. The parent
   searches, so titles and thumbnails are written for them.

## How the songs and narration sound
- Simple words, mostly one or two syllables. Lots of repetition: a chorus the child can learn in one listen.
- Warm, bright and slow. Clear pronunciation, standard American English.
- Every song teaches one thing: counting, colors, shapes, ABCs, animals, body parts, manners,
  routines (brushing teeth, bedtime, bath), or safety (crossing the road, washing hands).
- Characters say each other's names often, so kids learn who is who.
- Positive framing: show the right behaviour, don't scold.

## Words and things I never use
- Nothing scary, violent or mean. No villains, no crying left unresolved, no danger without a safe ending.
- No brand names, no products, no "ask your parents to buy".
- No calls to action aimed at kids (no "subscribe", "like", "comment"). Made for Kids rules.
- No copying of any existing channel's characters, names, catchphrases, designs or song arrangements.

## What I will not claim
- No claims about child development ("boosts IQ", "proven to help speech") without a named source.
- Never present an AI-made song as a traditional rhyme, or a traditional rhyme as our own.

## My format
- Main revenue driver: **compilations of 30–60 min** (several songs stitched, with chapters).
- Single songs: 2–4 min.
- Shorts: 15–30 sec chorus clips with a clear action ("Clap, clap, clap!").
- Look: bright 3D, soft rounded shapes, high-contrast primary colors, slow camera, no fast flashing cuts.
- Every video has a clear mini-story: a start, a lesson and a happy ending.
- Audience setting on YouTube: **Made for Kids = ON**.
