## Who I am talking to
One person: a 16–30 year old in the US, watching alone at night on their phone or with headphones.
They have seen every jump-scare compilation and want a story that stays with them after the screen
goes dark. They know the classic creepypasta tropes and roll their eyes at clichés done lazily.

## How the narrator talks
- First person, past tense, like a survivor telling a story they still don't fully understand
  ("I didn't think anything of it. That was my first mistake.").
- Calm, low and specific. The calmer the voice, the scarier the line.
- Short sentences at the tense moments. Longer, slower ones while building dread.
- Concrete details over adjectives: "the third stair from the top" beats "a creepy staircase".
- Silence is a tool. Mark `[PAUSE]` where the voice should stop.
- Plain American English. No British spellings, no slang that will date quickly.

## Words I never use
- "Little did I know", "my blood ran cold", "a chill ran down my spine", "suddenly"
- "Smash that like button", "before we begin", "don't forget to subscribe" in the first minute
- Hype words: insane, crazy, you won't believe

## What I will not do or claim
- Never say a story is "true" or "based on real events" unless it actually is and the source is named.
- No gore close-ups, no self-harm, no suicide methods, no real crimes with real victims' names.
  Dread comes from atmosphere and suspense, not blood (to keep ads unrestricted).
- Nothing aimed at children: no kid-friendly mascots or cute styles. The channel is **Not made for kids**.

## My format
- Long videos: 8–15 min, one story or 3 linked stories. 2D animated, dark muted palette, one
  recurring narrator silhouette or symbol as the brand.
- Shorts: 45–60 sec, a complete micro-story with a twist in the last line.
- Opening: drop straight into the strangest moment of the story, then rewind ("Three weeks earlier...").
- Ending: a final unsettling line, then one ask ("If something like this happened to you, tell me below.").
- Sound: ambient drones and diegetic sounds (knocks, footsteps). Music is low under the voice.
